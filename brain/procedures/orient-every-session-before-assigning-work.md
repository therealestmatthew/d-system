## The rule

When a coordinator resumes with cleared peer sessions, every session receives its full starter —
the shared contract and its own role section, as the governing starter-message prompt sets them
out — shown to the owner, approved, sent, and acknowledged, **before any assignment is sent to
anyone.** A role paragraph folded into an assignment brief is not a starter, and a session that
was never oriented does not know the lock, the slots, the merge gate or the message contract.

Two questions catch the slip:

1. **Has this session acknowledged a starter since it was last cleared?** If not, it has no role;
   send the starter, wait for the acknowledgement, then assign.
2. **Did every session in the roster get one, or only the ones I happened to need first?** The
   sessions not needed first are the ones that will be surprised later.

## Why

On 2026-09-25 the Session Manager resumed a multi-session build and sent the shared contract only
to the two builders it assigned first, bundled with their assignments. A third session later
received its role text welded to an assignment; four sessions received nothing. The owner said the
omission caused confusion and asked, on 2026-09-26, that the resume procedure make the kickoff the
first step. The kickoff procedure already existed and already said this; it was skipped because the
work seemed urgent and the sessions seemed to remember.

## How to apply

- The resume file for a coordinator names the kickoff as step 1, ahead of any assignment, and
  the assignment step says "only when every acknowledgement is recorded".
- Starters are composed from the governing prompt verbatim, with only the amendments the owner
  has ruled since (such as the concurrent-claim limit), and contain no assignment.
- The board records each acknowledgement; an assignment to a session with no recorded
  acknowledgement is the signal that the rule is being broken.
