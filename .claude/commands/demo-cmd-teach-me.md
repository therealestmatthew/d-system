# Teach me

$ARGUMENTS

Teach the topic above in tiers, then set an exercise. The tiers let the reader stop where they are
satisfied instead of reading to the end to find their level.

## The tiers

Write each tier under its own heading, in this order, and let the reader stop after any of them.

1. **In one paragraph.** What it is and why anyone cares. No terminology that is not explained in
   the same sentence it appears in.
2. **How it actually works.** The mechanism — what happens, in what order, and what depends on what.
   This is where most readers will stop, so it must stand on its own.
3. **Where it gets hard.** The parts people get wrong, the cases that break the simple version, the
   distinctions that look pedantic and are not.
4. **What it connects to.** The neighbouring ideas, and how to tell this one from the ones it is
   commonly confused with.

State at the top that the tiers deepen and that stopping early is expected. A reader who does not
know they may stop reads the whole thing anxiously.

## End with an exercise, not a summary

The final section is something the reader **does**, and it is what separates this from an article.

- It uses something the reader already has — their own work, their own material, their own
  situation. Do not invent a dataset or a scenario for them.
- It is small enough to finish in a few minutes.
- **Say what a good result looks like**, so they can tell whether they got it right without asking.
- **Say what the most likely wrong turn is** and how they will recognise it.

A recap of what was just said is not an exercise. If the topic genuinely does not admit a hands-on
step, say so plainly and set the closest thing that is genuinely active — a judgement to make, a
question to answer about their own situation.

## Never

- **Never assume a domain.** The topic is whatever was passed in, and the explanation carries no
  worked example of its own.
- **Never use an unexplained term**, including in the first tier where the temptation is strongest.
- **Never pad a tier to make the ladder look even.** A short tier is a short tier.
- **Never write a file.** Not `CLAUDE.md`, not `AGENTS.md`, not any file in the repository.
