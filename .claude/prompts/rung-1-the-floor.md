# Rung 1 — the floor

**The ingredient this rung adds: nothing.** This is the baseline every later rung is measured
against, and its job is to be genuinely representative rather than a strawman.

## What to type

A bare, under-specified ask about the engagement you brought — the kind you would actually type on a
Monday morning when you want something started and have not yet decided what you want. One line. No
background, no goal, no audience, no constraint.

The test of a good rung-1 prompt is that you recognise your own habit in it. If the room watches you
type something nobody would ever send, the whole ladder becomes a performance about a mistake nobody
makes, and rung 2 has nothing to improve on.

Do not sandbag it either. Writing something deliberately terrible is the same failure in the other
direction.

## What to watch for

You will get fluent, well-organised, confident text. That is the point. **Fluent text reads as good
text**, which is exactly why this failure survives in real work.

Name the failure out loud rather than letting the room infer it:

- **The advice would fit any client in this situation, and any year.** Nothing in it could only have
  been written about the engagement you are actually on.
- **It is not wrong.** There is nothing to correct, which is why nobody pushes back on output like
  this.
- **It ends where your work starts.** Every genuinely hard decision is still open.

Try this: read one recommendation aloud and ask the room which client it is about. Nobody can tell.
That lands better than any adjective.

## What a consultant really does next

This is the part worth saying explicitly, because it is the habit the ladder exists to interrupt.

They do not iterate. They skim the output, conclude the tool is not useful for real work, close it,
and write the thing themselves. The tool gets one attempt and fails an exam nobody told it it was
sitting.

Everything that follows is the case that the attempt failed because of what was withheld, not
because of what the model can do.

## If it does not go this way

If the output comes back genuinely specific and useful, do not argue with it. Say so, and say what
that means: something in your one-liner carried more than you intended — a name, a term of art, a
number — and the model used it. Ask the room to identify what did the work. That is the same lesson
arriving early, and it is more convincing than the planned version because nobody scripted it.
