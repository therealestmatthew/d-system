# Rung 5 — the interview, where the constraint arrives

**The ingredient this rung adds: the model's own questions.** You add no new information to the
prompt. You stop supplying and let it ask.

This is the rung the whole reordering exists for, and the only one where the kit's central claim is
demonstrated rather than described.

## Before you run it: hold something back

**This rung cannot succeed unless there is something for the model to find.**

Going into the ladder, pick one real obstacle about a person — someone whose agreement the work
needs and will not easily get, someone whose role makes them a bottleneck, someone whose past
experience makes them resistant — and **deliberately do not state it** in rungs 1 through 4. Not in
the situation, not in the objective, not in the audience description.

That withholding is the setup for this rung. Decide it before you start, not when you arrive here.

If you have already mentioned it, say so and run the rung anyway on something else you held back.
Running it with nothing withheld produces a model asking questions you have already answered, which
teaches the opposite of the intended lesson.

## What to type

Keep rung 4's prompt and add only an instruction to interview you first — or invoke the kit's
`demo-cmd-askme`, which is this instruction made permanent.

Then **answer the questions honestly**, including the one that reaches the obstacle. The constraint
enters the conversation as your answer to the model's question, never as something you typed
unprompted. That sequence is the entire point: the model found the thing that actually governs the
work, and it found it by asking.

## The change to watch for

Not a better-worded plan. **The recommendation inverting.**

A plan that routed around the person becomes a plan that makes them part of it. The sequencing
changes — the step that was third becomes first, because it is the one that earns the agreement
everything else depends on. Work that was scheduled by logical dependency gets rescheduled by human
dependency.

Say this before you answer the questions, so the room is watching for the inversion rather than
reading for style.

The wider lesson worth naming while the two outputs are on screen: the model was not withholding
this capability at rung 1. It never had the information, and it had no way to ask for it, because
nobody gave it the turn.

## If the model asks about systems and process instead of people

This happens, and the rung still works — but it teaches something weaker, so say so rather than
pretending.

What to do: answer the process questions, then say plainly that the questions stopped short of the
thing that actually governs this engagement, and volunteer the obstacle yourself. Then name the
difference — that a constraint you had to volunteer is worth less than one the model surfaced,
because in real use you would not have known to volunteer it.

The honest version of this failure is more useful to the room than a rehearsed success. It also
tells them exactly what to check for in their own use: whether the questions reached the people or
stopped at the process.
