# Rung 3 — adds the objective

**The ingredient this rung adds: the objective.** Where things stand now and where they need to get
to. Nothing else changes from rung 2.

## What to add

Keep rung 2's prompt exactly as it was and add the goal:

- **Current state** — where the engagement actually is against the thing you care about.
- **Target state** — where it needs to be, and by when if a date genuinely exists.

**The objective only.** Still no audience, still nothing about how any individual will react. Those
belong to rungs 4 and 5.

## The change to watch for

This is usually the largest single jump in the ladder, and it has a describable shape:

**Advice in general becomes advice about a specific gap.** The output stops surveying the territory
and starts arguing about how to close the distance between two stated points. Recommendations
acquire sequence, because closing a gap has an order. Tradeoffs appear, because a target makes some
paths too slow and others too expensive.

The sharpest test: the output now contains something a consultant could **disagree** with on
professional grounds. Rung 1 offered nothing to argue with. If someone in the room wants to push
back on a recommendation, that is the rung working.

## State the expectation before you run it

Tell the room, in advance, that you expect this rung to outperform rung 2 by more than rung 2
outperformed rung 1.

Stating a prediction and then testing it in front of people teaches more than asserting a result
afterwards, because the room can see you were not free to reinterpret whatever happened as a
success. It also means you have to be willing to be wrong in public, which is what makes the claim
worth anything.

## Warn about false precision

An objective invites the model to build on the number you gave it. Confident plans, staged
milestones, quantified trajectories — all resting on a figure you may have supplied casually.

This is genuinely useful and it is also the moment to check:

- **Is the number real, or the one everybody repeats?**
- **Is the target yours, or inherited from someone who set it under different assumptions?**
- **Does the plan degrade gracefully if the number is wrong by a third?**

Say this out loud while the confident output is on screen. The model will not volunteer that its
precision is borrowed from yours.

## If it does not go this way

If rung 3 does not clearly beat rung 2, do not paper over it. Say the prediction failed and check
the usual cause first: an objective stated so broadly that it added no constraint. Tighten it to a
specific gap and run once more. If it still does not move, say that plainly — a ladder that reports
one honest failure is more trustworthy than one that reports five successes.
