# Rung 2 — adds the situation

**The ingredient this rung adds: the situation.** Who is involved, what the arrangement is, and what
constrains it structurally. Nothing else changes from rung 1.

## What to add

Take your rung-1 line and add the circumstances:

- Who the parties are and how they relate — structurally, by role and arrangement.
- What the arrangement actually is: what exists today, what is in place, what was inherited.
- What constrains it: contractual, structural, systemic, financial.

**Situation only.** This rung leaks if you let anything else in:

- **No objective.** Do not say what you are trying to achieve, where you want to get to, or what
  good looks like. That is rung 3, and stating it here collapses two rungs into one.
- **No target figure.** A number implies the goal.
- **No audience.** That is rung 4.
- **Nothing about a person being difficult, resistant, or a problem.** Not their attitude, not their
  history, not what they will say. The whole ladder is built so that arrives in rung 5, and only as
  an answer to a question the model asked. Saying it here is the one mistake that cannot be undone
  later in the run.

The split only works if this rung is disciplined about what it withholds. The discipline is the
teaching.

## The comparison to make against rung 1

The ladder teaches by difference. No single output proves anything, so say what changed:

- Does the advice now refer to **this** arrangement, or is it still advice about arrangements in
  general?
- Has anything dropped out that was in rung 1 — a recommendation that no longer applies now that the
  constraints are known?
- Is anything now **wrong** rather than merely generic? That is progress: a claim specific enough to
  be wrong is a claim specific enough to argue with.

## Say out loud that the gain is modest

Expect a smaller improvement than the room is anticipating. Situation alone usually moves the answer
less than the objective will in rung 3.

**Say so.** A ladder that oversells its second rung loses the room for its third, because the
audience starts discounting every claim you make about the next step. Naming the modest gain in
advance buys the credibility that rung 3 needs.

## Name what the model still does not know

Close the rung by listing it, because this sets up everything that follows:

- What you are actually trying to achieve.
- Who is going to read the output and what they will do with it.
- Anything about the people involved beyond their roles.

Say that the third one is the interesting one, and leave it there.

## If it does not go this way

If the output barely changes, that is a legitimate result — say so rather than hunting for an
improvement to point at. It means the situation you supplied was structural detail the model could
not use without knowing the goal. That is precisely rung 3's argument, arriving with evidence.
