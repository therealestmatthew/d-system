Idea Realization System

One pipeline from an idea spoken in conversation to a delivered capability verified against that idea — nine stages, five owner gates, and everything between them automated with an audit trail.

The five gates

decision categories, not interruptions

A gate is a category of decision reserved to the owner — not necessarily a separate sitting. Gate items queue and arrive decision-ready: the proposal, the adversarial findings against it, and the evidence, pre-digested. Throughput of the whole machine is set by the owner's decision throughput, so gate presentation is a first-class design concern.

G1

Idea approval

An idea enters the append-only log. Per idea, in conversation — as today.

G2

Track acceptance

The partition's tracks and new-plan-vs-amendment rulings stand. One sitting per partition run.

G3

Plan approval

A plan, its phases and their proposed ordering, after adversarial review. Ranking next_up stays here, with the owner.

G4

Integration

A branch merges into dev. Enforced at the tool boundary by the capability broker — a run cannot merge itself.

G5

Completion review

Batched: the owner clears a queue of finished phases in one sitting, each individually closed through /session-close.

Division of labor

the authority model
Owner-reserved, permanently
  • Idea approval and lineage annotations
  • Partition acceptance and plan approval
  • next_up ranking and all cross-track priority
  • Integration into dev; phase completion via /session-close
  • Edits to AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md; deletions under _working/; anything _private/
Agent-writable, always audited
  • Idea finding annotations and permitted status moves
  • Draft documents; branch commits
  • depends_on and systems locks within an approved plan
  • Run-ledger entries for every transition
  • Proposals — orderings, splits, delivered — that a gate disposes of

Every role carries a written contract — inputs, outputs, and a binding never-do column: triage never advances past triaged; validators never edit; the planner drafts and does not decide; validators see the requirement and the diff, never the developer's rationale.

Orchestration principles

ADR-018

Thin state, repo wins

Graph state carries run identity, stage and references — never record content. On disagreement the repository is authoritative and the run re-derives.

Checkpoints cover crashes

Resume handles process death only. A validator's no or an adversary's blocker follows the stage's written failure path — revise, escalate, park. No stage wedges silently.

Budgets and a kill switch

Every run carries a token budget with a hard cap that parks rather than exceeds; one owner-operable switch halts all dispatch.

Evidence over persona

Every agent is the same model, so independence comes from executed tests and repository checks, not persona prompts — plus owner spot-audits of passed validations at G5.

Measured, not vibed

Automation rate, cycle time and rework rate computed deterministically from the ledger and log; baselines recorded before targets are set.

It learns between runs

Rejections, recurring findings and realization outcomes feed the anti-pattern store; the plan-quality standard cites the store when revised.
