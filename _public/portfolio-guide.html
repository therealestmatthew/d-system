1 · At a glance

A ledger of promises, people and projects, with the analysis still to come

The personal productivity system is the secondary purpose of D-System: tracking the owner's people, projects, commitments and tasks, plus the tags that connect them. Records are plain JSON files, validated against schemas, loaded into a DuckDB database that is rebuilt on demand. Real records live in a private, untracked folder; the tracked repository holds a small fictional example set so every tool can be tested without exposing anything.

The capture pipeline that turns a rough note into a validated record is built and tested. The analysis layer that was meant to sit on top of the records, six signals and four syntheses such as a stale-project radar and a weekly review, is planned and unbuilt. Nothing serves the records to a screen yet: there are no API routes and no UI views for the four core entities. The single most important open question is whether the real portfolio holds any people or commitments at all, which only the owner can answer.

The one blocker

Fourteen of the twenty-five open phases wait, directly or through a dependency chain, on seeding the portfolio through the capture pipeline (phase-cap-08) and on the portfolio data question (phase-proj-01): do people and commitments hold real records, and if not, is that friction or no need? Until those two are answered, the syntheses, the ledger and the projections build against a one-commitment example set. The other eleven wait on the reliability track's atomic rebuild or are ready now.

2 · The records

Nine record types, five of them core

Each record type is a JSON Schema under schemas/ and a table in the database. Open a card for the fields that matter: required values, status vocabularies, dates and links to other records. Every schema rejects unknown fields, so adding a field is a schema change.

Project core5 example records

A unit of work or attention with a lifecycle. It is the hub the other records attach to.

  • Required: id, name, status, category, type.
  • Status: active · inactive · planning · blocked · complete · archived. Category: work · learning · system · personal. Type: project · activity · goal · system · certification.
  • Dates: started, target_date, last_reviewed. A review cadence (daily to quarterly, or ad-hoc) says how often it should be looked at. last_touched is derived at rebuild, never stored.
  • Links: tags (tag ids) and stakeholders (person ids). The project side is the intended authority for membership (phase-rel-04, queued).
Person core1 example record

A canonical individual. Today it is deliberately thin: name, optional role and contact details, and the projects they belong to.

  • Required: id, name. No status and no dates.
  • Links: projects (project ids). Commitments point at a person through promised_to; waiting-on records through owed_by.
  • Planned extensions (draft architecture ARCH-007): companies, time-bounded affiliations, typed aliases such as several email addresses resolving to one person, and project parties with dated roles.
Commitment core1 example record

A promise made to someone, or to yourself. The record the whole system exists to keep honest.

  • Required: id (c-N), description, status, priority, created.
  • Status: open · in_progress · complete · blocked · cancelled. Priority: high · medium · low.
  • Dates: created (required), due_date, completed. There is no start date and no estimate.
  • Links: project_id (optional), promised_to (a person id, the name as written, or null for a promise to yourself), tags, and the raw capture it came from. Tasks point back at it; they are not embedded.
  • Protected fields: promised_to, due_date and completed may never be invented by an agent during capture. They come from the owner's words or stay empty.
Task core3 example records

A step of work. It may hang off a commitment, a project, both, or neither.

  • Required: id (t-N), description, status, created.
  • Status: open · in_progress · complete · blocked · cancelled. Priority: high · medium · low, or none.
  • Dates: created, due_date, completed. No start date, no duration.
  • Links: commitment_id and project_id, both optional. Parentless records appear in the derived unfiled view so they are never lost.
Tag core29 in the shared taxonomy

The shared vocabulary. Tags are the one record always read from the tracked repository, whichever data root is active.

  • Required: id, label, category. Category: client · platform · tech · domain · methodology · context.
  • Links: related tags. A deprecated flag retires a tag without deleting it.
  • Every tag a project uses must already exist in _data/tags.json; the rebuild refuses an unknown tag.
Interaction, Decision, Waiting-on, Development event supportingno example records yet

Four record types added in 2026-09 when the capture system was defined. They have schemas, tables and tests, but the fictional example set holds none of them yet.

  • Interaction: a meeting, call, email, message or conversation on a date, with confirmed participants and unresolved participant names kept apart.
  • Decision: what was decided, the rationale and alternatives, with a status of active, superseded or reversed.
  • Waiting-on: something owed to you. Dates for requested, last_chased, due and received; status open, chased, received or abandoned. The requested date is the clock for "gone quiet".
  • Development event: a dated learning or milestone event with hours. Its milestone type is the only milestone-shaped field in the system.

Why capture is strict about evidence

The capture routing decision (ADR-007) and the capture requirements (REQ-002) set three rules. A raw note is written before any interpretation and is never changed. Every agent-filled field carries provenance: a verbatim quote with its offset, scored explicit, inferred or guessed. Four fields can never be invented: who a promise was made to, when it is due, the text of a decision, and a completion. A record whose stakes are high or whose evidence is weak is flagged for the owner; a record that would create a new person, project or tag is held until the owner takes the identity decision. This is what makes it safe to let an agent read a messy note.

Where the real data lives, and how the tools find it

The structure-content boundary decision (ADR-009) separates what is tracked from what is private. The repository tracks the schemas, tools and a fictional example set under _data/. The owner's real records live under _private/portfolio/, which is gitignored and absent from every worktree and every cloud session. Tools find the real data through one environment variable:

D_SYSTEM_DATA_ROOT=_private/portfolio uv run python tools/rebuild_db.py

Tags and the idea log are always read from the tracked tree regardless of the override. A fresh clone with nothing set must still pass the tests and a rebuild, and does.

What the database holds today

Sixteen tables: projects, people, project_people, tags, project_tags, commitments, tasks, interactions, decisions, waiting_on, development_events, memories, and four idea tables. Three views: project activity, the derived last_touched per project, and unfiled for parentless records. The rebuild validates every source file first and writes nothing if any file fails. None of the six planned signal views exists; a search of the source for their names returns nothing.

4 · Using it today

Five operations, all from the command line

There is no screen for the records yet. Everything below is a terminal command run from the repository, with the data root pointed at the private portfolio for real work.

Capture a note
uv run python tools/capture.py "Promised Jordan the vendor shortlist by Friday"
uv run python tools/capture.py --inbox     # convert every file dropped in _capture/inbox/

Writes one raw record under _capture/raw/. Nothing is interpreted at this step. Operations document: OPS-008.

Review and promote staged records
uv run python tools/review.py show
uv run python tools/review.py promote --clean          # every clean record in one action
uv run python tools/review.py promote staged-… --set due_date=2026-10-17
uv run python tools/review.py create staged-…          # take the identity decision for a held person or project
uv run python tools/review.py discard staged-…
uv run python tools/review.py correct commitment c-12 due_date 2026-10-24 --reason "date moved"

Promotion is the only path into the data root. Corrections append a dated entry to the promoted record; the raw capture is never touched.

Gap worth knowing: the structuring step between a raw note and a staged record is a library function an agent calls. No command-line entry point exists for it, so today a session or an agent runs it, not the owner by hand.

Add a project directly

The written procedure (brain/procedures/add-new-project.md): create projects/<id>.json under the data root with id, name, status, category, type, tags, stakeholders and a review cadence; add any missing tag to _data/tags.json first; rebuild; verify the row with a query. The id is permanent. There is no equivalent written procedure for adding a person; today a person is created through a held capture or by writing the JSON by hand.

Rebuild the database
uv run python tools/rebuild_db.py                                   # example set
D_SYSTEM_DATA_ROOT=_private/portfolio uv run python tools/rebuild_db.py   # real records

Validates every source, drops and recreates all tables and views, writes data/d_system.duckdb. Operations document: OPS-002, whose prose still says "eight tables"; the code creates sixteen.

Load context into a session
uv run python tools/load_context.py --project example-web-platform
uv run python tools/load_context.py -q "vendor" --type decision --limit 5

Prints Markdown to paste into a model that has no repository access. It reads only the memories table, meaning the durable notes under brain/; it does not load projects, people, commitments or tasks. A project context pack that would (phase-syn-01) is queued.

7 · Templates today

Four families, none for portfolio data

The template library (templates/html/library.yaml) declares every HTML template, its family and how it is populated: slot-fill (every slot filled by code), ai-adaptation (an agent adapts it) or both. A page can be read with scripts disabled, follows light and dark mode in CSS alone, and takes every colour from one token file. None of the fifteen templates reads a project, person, commitment or task.

House the approved stylehouse-page, house-components

The house style the owner approved on 2026-09-30 (REQ-036): a magazine default with Swiss and mono variants that change type, spacing and rules only. Five components: stat row, columns, callout, pill and table. No JavaScript. Generators: the engine pages and the house CSS specimen. This guide is set in it.

Overview overview-*.html, 8 templates

Embeddable data panels for the live-demo overview page, generated by tools/generate_overview.py from the governance corpus: metrics, systems, concepts, terms. Slot-fill, no script, scoped under .overview.

Atlas atlas-page, atlas-components

Standalone showcase and architecture pages with an interactive shell: sidebar navigation, collapsible sections, focusable SVG diagrams. The one family that ships script. Population is both: the shell slots are mechanical, the masthead, sections and page script are an author's.

Lit-report lit-report-*.html, 3 templates

The literature-review campaign report: an index, a deliverable page and a browsable table rendered by tools/lit_report_render.py. Slot-fill.

Constraints any new portfolio template inherits
  • House family by default; no colour literals, every value a token; status is always one of four pills; one accent.
  • No script in house pages. Native <details> is not script and is already used in the overview family.
  • Declared in library.yaml as a file or a new pattern; the library refuses an undeclared file.
  • Deterministic: same commit, same bytes. An "overdue" or "today" marker therefore needs an explicit as-of date passed in, not the wall clock.
  • Generated pages contain no confidential identifier; build against the example set, render real data only to a gitignored location.
  • No generator reads portfolio data yet. The first template pays for a shared loader that uses the data root.
8 · Template proposals

Timeline and Gantt, and twelve more the data could feed

The owner named a simple timeline and a Gantt chart. A design agent assessed both against the house constraints and the schemas, then proposed twelve more. The short version: the timeline is buildable today; a Gantt is buildable at project level today and needs a start-date field for tasks; the cheapest first build is a commitments ledger, because it needs no new component and proves the data loader everything else shares. Each proposal is recorded in the idea log as 000673 (timeline) through 000686 (RACI matrix), with 000687 (the shared loader and fixtures) and 000688 (the stale digest-export dependency), so they survive into triage.

Simple timeline data readyeffort M · house · slot-fill

What it shows. A per-project vertical timeline of dated events: started, last reviewed, each commitment's created, due and completed dates, each task's, the target date, and later interactions, decisions and milestone events. The example web platform project yields about twelve events today. A portfolio-wide view is the same component with a project column.

How. A CSS-only vertical list with a left rule and markers, chronological rather than proportional, which suits sparse personal data. One new component in the house stylesheet, tokens only. Inline SVG only if proportional spacing is wanted.

Decisions for the owner: per-project, portfolio or person view first; chronological or proportional; whether created dates count as events; whether a milestone development event is an acceptable milestone source; shared component or page-local CSS; as-of date from a parameter or the commit date.

Gantt chart project level only todayeffort M, L with tasks · house · slot-fill

Data readiness. Projects have started and target_date: three of the five example projects draw as bars, one is open-ended, one unscheduled. Commitments and tasks have no start date, no duration and no dependency field, so a task-level bar from created to due_date would show lead time, not a schedule. Minimal schema change for a real task Gantt: a nullable start_date on task and commitment, with the matching column and loader change. Dependencies are planned as a separate work-dependency record in the draft data model, so an interim field on tasks would cut across that direction.

How. CSS grid, one row per item and one column per week or month, bars placed by inline grid-column; labels stay real text, dark mode is free, the table wrapper gives horizontal scroll on a phone. SVG is day-accurate and can draw dependency arrows but wraps no text; a hybrid adds an SVG arrow overlay once dependency data exists.

No-script consequences. No drag, zoom or live filter. Zoom becomes separate renders at week, month and quarter. The "today" line is the as-of date stamped on the page. Interaction belongs in the workbench, which is the live view.

Decisions for the owner: accept project level first; add start_date to tasks, commitments or both; interim dependency field or wait for the work-dependency record; how to show open-ended and unscheduled projects; what colour encodes, since the status colours are taken.

Suggested build order
  1. Shared loader and a richer fictional fixture. Not a template. Reads the data root, takes an as-of date, and adds example records covering overdue, blocked, a milestone, an interaction and a waiting-on item. The current set shows exactly one overdue task.
  2. Commitments ledger. Data ready, no new CSS; proves the loader and the as-of handling.
  3. Portfolio dashboard. Same components plus counts by status and cadence.
  4. Project brief and person card. First new component, the card. After the owner answers the data question, in case people records do not exist.
  5. Timeline. One new component; data ready for the per-project view.
  6. Agenda, then kanban. Reuse the card.
  7. Project-level Gantt. Grid-based, no schema change. The start-date change only after the owner decides.
  8. Waiting-on board and decision register. Easy, but need fixtures.
  9. Stale radar page, velocity trend, weekly review. After the signal views and snapshots land, so they read views instead of recomputing.
  10. RACI. Last; blocked on the draft data model, which has no phase.

Each step adds at most one new component, and nothing that needs a schema or a view comes before the templates that read today's fields.

9 · Decisions for the owner

What only you can settle

  1. The portfolio data question. Do people and commitments hold real records under the private root? If not, is that tooling friction or no current need? The command that produces the evidence is in section 3. Everything downstream is sized by the answer (phase-proj-01).
  2. Which template first. The recommendation is the shared loader, then the commitments ledger. Timeline and Gantt follow in the build order above unless you want them moved up.
  3. Timeline shape. Per-project, portfolio-wide or per-person first; chronological or proportional spacing; whether record-creation dates count as events.
  4. Gantt scope. Accept project level as the first version, or add a start_date field to tasks and commitments now, and decide between an interim dependency field and the planned work-dependency record.
  5. The as-of date rule. Pages are deterministic by requirement. Does a portfolio page take its as-of date from a parameter, or from the source commit date?
  6. Re-scope the digest export (phase-syn-05) from the cancelled page pipeline to the house family before anyone claims it.
  7. The structuring command gap. Whether the owner should be able to run the structuring step from the command line, or whether it stays an agent-only step by design.
  8. The single-session protocol drafted this session (GOV-022) lists six open questions of its own, starting with whether the Haiku-default model rule is standing.
